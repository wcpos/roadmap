The desktop's top edge — what the apps on your dock actually do with it

Nobody good leaves the title strip empty or writes a title in it. Finder, Mail, VS Code, Spotify, Slack, Linear, Figma, Arc all treat the band under the window controls as the most valuable row on screen: it is always visible, always at the top, and the OS already made it a drag handle. Four idioms below, each with the app that owns it, drawn on the till at lg in a mac and a Windows window. ← the first three · phone and md

4 · Unified toolbar — the search lives in the strip

macOS's own idiom since Big Sur: the toolbar and the title bar are one row (Finder, Mail, Notes, Safari), and VS Code's command centre does the same on every platform. The products search field moves up into the strip, centred, with the scan target and the list/sliders icons beside it; the products column then starts straight at the chip row. The strip is no longer a cost — it gives back the 56 pt search row, so the grid gains a row net. The cart bar stays as it is; the rail runs full height under the lights.

References: Finder / Mail unified toolbar · VS Code command centre · Spotify's top search.

5 · Command centre — one field that finds anything, scans anything

Option 4's field, but it is not the products search: it is the till's search. Type a name → products; a number → an order; a name with an @ → a customer; scan → the barcode lands here wherever focus was. That is what the register prototype's "scanner captured anywhere" promised and never wired (research #342), and it is the Raycast / Spotlight / VS Code ⌘K pattern every desktop user already has in their hands. The products column keeps its chips; the search row goes, as in 4. The strip's right end carries the sync/online dot and the bell, so the cart bar is the register alone.

References: VS Code command centre · Raycast · Linear ⌘K · Spotify's search as the whole top bar.

6 · Open orders as window tabs

Figma, Arc, every browser: the strip is a tab strip. The till's open orders — the customer who stepped away, the table still eating — become tabs across the top, each with its line count; + starts a new one. The cart column then shows one order and loses its own open-orders control (the prototype's takeover, decision C26, becomes the strip). It is the most desktop-native of the six and the only one that changes a workflow rather than a row: switching orders is a click at the top, visible all day, with no menu.

References: Figma file tabs · Arc / Chrome tab strip · Things and Craft's window tabs on macOS.

7 · The strip is the register

Xcode's activity bar and Slack's workspace header: the strip carries the state of the thing you are running. The register bar — Front counter · Open · £480.80 in drawer, the drawer icon, the bell, the sync dot — moves up into the strip, full width, and the cart column starts directly with the customer row. The register was already the E ruling's "one bar above the cart"; this just says the window's top edge is that bar, which is what a cashier glances at all day. Nothing new is invented; one row is removed from the cart column and the strip is the most important row on screen.

References: Xcode's activity viewer · Slack's workspace bar · Discord's channel header in the title strip · Linear's breadcrumb strip.

Where I land now: 7, with 5's field as the next step. The strip should carry the thing a cashier glances at all day — the register and its state — because that is what the top edge is for in every serious desktop app, and it removes a row from the cart instead of adding a band. It changes nothing about the register model (E is still "one bar above the cart"; the bar just becomes the window's edge) and it is one component moving up, not a new one. 5 is the bigger idea — one field that finds a product, an order or a customer and catches every scan — and it belongs in the same strip on the left once the register sits there; do it second, as the search work it is. 4 alone under-uses the strip (it is 5 without the ambition); 6 is a real workflow change (open orders as tabs) that deserves its own decision on the open-orders prototype, not a ride on this one. On the web app and the tablet, where there is no strip, 7 is simply the bar it already is — so nothing forks between platforms; the desktop just gets its bar at the very top.
