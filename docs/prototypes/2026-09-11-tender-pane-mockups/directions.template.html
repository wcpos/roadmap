Five directions for the pay step

Each is a different structure, not a polish of the tile grid. Mobbin has no POS apps, so the references are the payment moments people already like in consumer apps: Cash App, Apple Pay, Monzo, Revolut, Blackbird, Chase. Point at one, or at pieces of several, and I'll build that one properly.

01 The keypad is the screen

Cash App's idea: the amount is the whole surface. Tap Pay and the left column becomes a dark keypad with the balance already typed. The method is the last tap and it is the commit: Cash takes 18,00, or type 20 first and the change appears. Split and terminals are behind More.

Steals

Giant type, keys without boxes, method-as-commit. Colour flash on commit (Cash App goes green).

Costs

Method-last is the amount-first order you rejected for split. Six gateways live behind More. Dark pane inside a light app needs the dark theme to be real.

Where it fits

Cash-heavy counters, markets, fast lines. Card-only shops get one extra tap versus a Card-first design.

02 The wallet sheet

Apple Pay's idea: nothing navigates. The POS stays where it is; a sheet rises with the amount, a row of method cards showing each terminal's live status, and one confirm. Captured, the sheet becomes a tick and dismisses. Split is Add another payment inside the sheet.

Steals

No column swap, no navigation. Method cards carry terminal status. The tick is the receipt of the moment.

Costs

Sheets are what the design systems withdraw for long tasks: split-with-three-legs and the Legacy webview strain it. Products behind the scrim are dead weight.

Where it fits

Single-tender sales, which are most sales. Pair with a fuller pane for the rare split or legacy case.

03 The receipt tape

Blackbird's idea: the check is the interface. The tender pane is the receipt that will print, and payments are lines you add to it. Tap Add payment, pick a method, the line appears with an editable amount and the keypad beside it. When the tape balances, it stamps PAID and prints.

Blackbird — one big stepper for the one number that changes
Blackbird — dark, high contrast, made for a counter at night

Steals

Payments as receipt lines, so split is just two lines. The PAID stamp as the completion beat. The tape prints as-is.

Costs

Monospace and a paper metaphor are a strong flavour; it must be done with conviction or it looks like a joke. The ledger pane on the right becomes redundant.

Where it fits

Shops that print every receipt and want the cashier to see exactly what the customer will get. Also the natural home for fiscal numbering and reprint counts.

04 No navigation at all

SumUp Lite and Toast Fast Cash: the products never leave. The cart's footer is the tender: two big buttons that take the exact balance, Cash 18,00 and Card 18,00, and a row for terminals, split and legacy. The leg lands in the cart. A sheet opens only for change, a custom amount or a split.

Steals

Zero transitions for the common sale. Products stay for the "and a bag?" add-on. The two exact-amount buttons are the Toast/Odoo shortcut the research already flagged.

Costs

The cart column is narrow, so six gateways collapse into Other terminal. The ledger view (payments taken, remaining) has to live inside the cart. Change-giving needs a sheet.

Where it fits

Small shops with cash and one terminal, which is most of the install base. It is the least "checkout" and the most "till".

05 Design the terminal moment, not the picker

Apple Pay and Chase spend their design on the moment money moves, not on the menu before it. Whatever picker survives, the state after Card is the screen the cashier stares at while the customer taps: one amount, the terminal's live steps, a way out, then the tick and three receipt buttons.

Steals

Status as a timeline the cashier can read at arm's length. Battery and connection on the terminal card. The tick and receipt choice as one card.

Costs

It is not a picker, so it composes with 01–04 rather than replacing them. Needs the driver to expose real steps, which the server and device legs partly do already.

Where it fits

Everywhere. This is where the "moment of joy" actually lives; the picker is just the door.
