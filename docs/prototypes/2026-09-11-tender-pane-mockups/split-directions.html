Five ways to split

Same pay surface, five different models of what a split is. The current build is A. The references are the split-bill idioms Mobbin has: Splitwise's mode switch, Revolut's Amount / Percent / Share tabs, GoPay's split-by-item, Blackbird's stepper, Monzo and Wise's per-person lists. Point at one, or at pieces.

A Even ways, plus amount, percent and share tabs

The current chooser widened. The sheet gets Revolut's three tabs: Ways (2 / 3 / 4 / 5 / 6, amount each), Amount (type the first leg), Percent (50 / 25 / 10 / type). Everything else stays: plan strip, per-leg methods, Change split.

Steals

Three ways to say the same plan, so the cashier picks the one the customer said: "half", "twenty quid", "thirty percent".

Costs

Three tabs is more to learn than one row of options. Percent is rare at a retail till.

Fits

Shops with deposits and lay-by, where a percent or a fixed first payment is the norm.

B Build the legs first, then take them in order

Plan-first. The split screen is a short list of legs: method and amount on each row, add a row, the balance line at the bottom must read zero. Then Take payments in order runs them one after another: cash keypad, then the terminal moment, then done. Nothing is taken until the plan balances.

Steals

The whole split is visible and editable before money moves. Uneven splits with three methods are natural.

Costs

Two screens for the common "half and half". Editing amounts in rows on a touch screen needs the keypad to target a row.

Fits

Group purchases, gift-card-plus-card, anything with three or more legs.

C Split by item

GoPay and every restaurant POS: tick the cart lines this customer is paying for, the amount becomes their sum, take it, the ticked lines grey out and the rest carry on. Even splits stay one tap away as a chip. The receipt shows who paid for what.

Steals

The split matches how customers actually ask: "I'll get the scarf". No arithmetic, no plan.

Costs

Needs the cart lines on the pay surface, so the ledger pane and this list overlap. Tax and discounts have to be apportioned per line, which is real work in the order model.

Fits

Cafés, markets, anything where several people buy together. Not needed for a single customer paying two ways.

D How many people?

Blackbird's stepper. One big number, minus and plus. "Between 3" gives three person cards, each with their share; tap a person, pick their method, take it, the card turns green. Uneven shares: tap a card and type. The vocabulary is people, not ways.

Steals

Zero chooser: the number of people is the split. Cards make progress visible without a strip.

Costs

"One customer paying half cash, half card" is a person count of 1, which reads oddly. Uneven shares need a second interaction.

Fits

Groups at a counter. Pairs well with C for the item case.

E No plan at all

Remove the concept. Every payment is just an amount; the split "control" is three chips in the helpers row — Half, A third, Type — that set the number. Take it, the balance drops, the legs taken appear under the number, the next chips are for the new balance. There is nothing to plan or change because there is no plan.

Steals

Nothing to learn. The keypad already does custom amounts; Half and A third are just two more chips. The screen never has a mode.

Costs

No preview of the whole split before the first payment. "Three ways" is Half-then-Half-of-the-rest, which is wrong arithmetic unless the chips are computed as thirds of the original.

Fits

The tie-breaker principle: Square and Lightspeed cashiers expect "type a smaller amount and take it". This is that, made obvious.
