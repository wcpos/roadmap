0 · The flow: what the cashier is meant to do

The question the screen answers: can I sell right now, and if not, what is the one thing I do?

Ruled 2026-09-11 (roadmap#214): registers are server records. Every store gets one by default; the device binds to it silently; a cashier never creates one. So after an upgrade from 1.11.x the cashier is meant to do nothing about registers at all. They see the Open register card (if register sessions are on) or the plain cart (if off).

EditionWhat the plugin does (on next, verified in code)
FreeHolds exactly one register in the wcpos_register option. Register_Store::ensure_free_default() mints it named "Register" the first time anything asks. Free can never reach "no register".
ProOne register per store: Registers::publish_store() seeds it when a store is published; backfill_store_registers() runs on activation and in updates/update-2.0.0.php for every published store. A store with no register after that is an anomaly, not a first-run state.

Why "AL Store" shows it anyway. The app reads GET /registers?store_id=N; an empty list is none, a failed request stays unknown. The main-lane plugins on the Local sites have no /registers route, so this screenshot is against a next server where that store genuinely has zero active registers: a store that is not publish (draft/private), a backfill that threw and was logged, or a register moved to another store in Pro's admin. One check settles it: GET /wp-json/wcpos/v1/registers?store_id=N.

3 · What this draws on

Rule 5, state is visible not explained One badge in one place at the top of the pane; a disabled control says why in a few words beside it; error/empty/trouble = one line + actions + docs link. That is the Notice and EmptyState contract, so 1A and 2A are those components used as designed.
Blue Bottle, "Cafe Closed" Mobbin. The closed state is a small red label above the place name, the bag stays visible and empty, the primary button is disabled and names the state. Borrowed by 1C (status beside the place) and by the bar pill in every frame.
Grill'd, closed restaurant Mobbin. Status pill under the name, then one tinted band saying ordering is unavailable and why. Menu stays browsable. The exact shape of 1A: browse, do not order.
StubHub, action required Mobbin. An amber band at the top of the list with the button inside it. The band carries the next action, not just the state. 1A's action and 1D.
Square web, inline banner + bottom action Mobbin. "You need to verify your identity before you can create bills" as a peach band; the one primary action pinned at the bottom. Same split as our band + the Open register card.
Revolut Business, connection issue Mobbin. Icon, three words, Try again, centred in the pane that failed. 2A and the "can't reach" variant.
Adobe Express / Base44, locked Mobbin · Mobbin. An outline lock, one line, one unlock action. The lock reads as "locked, not broken", which is why it stays in 1A, but from the Tabler outline family the rest of the screen uses.
Theory Progressive disclosure (the common case shows nothing: an open register has no band); proximity (the action sits in the band that explains it); one badge one place (the bar pill names the state once; the band only appears where a tap is refused); data-ink (no second explanatory line).

6 · WP Admin: how a store owner sets registers up

Question this surface answers: where do I set up my register(s), and what applies to which store? Most sites are one owner, one store, one register. The design has to be right for them first and still hold for a site with four stores and nine registers.

Today: three places, two copies of the same policy

WhereFreePro
Settings › General › RegisterRegister sessions · Variance threshold · Expected close timeSame fields, site-wide
Settings › Registers (its own page)Name · Default float of the one register, a stale "No registers have connected yet" notice, an Upgrade nudgeA flat list of every register: Name · Default float · Store select · Add register. No rename-in-place, no retire, no "open since"
Stores › store › Register—Register sessions · Variance threshold · Expected close time again, and a store value replaces General verbatim, blank included (no inheritance)

So the Free owner edits one register across two pages, and the Pro owner edits the same policy in two places with no rule about which wins beyond "the store's copy". The Registers page assigns registers to stores with a dropdown, which is the inverse of how anyone thinks about a shop.

What the category leaders do

AppShape
Lightspeed X-Series (Vend)Setup › Outlets and registers. Registers are created inside the outlet; adding an outlet ends with "what registers does this outlet need?" (help). The closest match to WooCommerce stores.
ShopifySettings › Locations lists locations with a POS column; POS is a property of the location. Cash tracking is toggled in the POS app; sessions are reviewed in admin per location (help). Mobbin
SquareSettings › Device management › Points of sale (a flat list, each with a location) plus Modes that carry cash settings such as default starting cash and apply to every device on the mode (help). Policy lives on a reusable profile, not on the location.
Stripe TerminalReaders are created into a location; the drawer says "Adding location to: All locations · 0 readers". Mobbin
HomerunDepartments and Locations as cards inside the company page, each with an inline "Add" row, no separate page. Mobbin

Theory: settings live on the thing they describe (a register is a property of a store; policy is a property of a store; in Free the store is the site). Progressive disclosure: one register has no name to manage, so Free never sees a name field. Defaults flow down and overrides are explicit (a "site default" chip beats a silent copy).

7 · The register settings themselves, redrawn

Question the section answers: what happens at my till when I switch this on? Today's three fields are the database columns with their underscores removed: "Variance threshold" (a money amount, unlabelled), "Expected close time" (HH:MM typed by hand), "Register sessions" (a noun, not a behaviour), and two tips that describe the format instead of the effect.
