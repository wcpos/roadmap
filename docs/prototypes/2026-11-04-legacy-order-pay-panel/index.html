Legacy order-pay panel: the terminal moment on the classic pay page

The "Legacy" tab in the 2.0 checkout is the classic WooCommerce pay page in an iframe, with Pro's shared order-pay panel inside the gateway's accordion row. Today it is three lines of text and a WordPress button row. The app's own terminal moment was decided on 2026-10-05 (horizontal stepper, one line in cashier words, Details unfolds the rows and ids). This board is the chosen way of bringing that decision onto the classic page, next to today's panel as the reference column.

Chosen (Paul, 2026-11-04): A, the terminal moment rehosted. "recommended looks good"
  • B (one line and a bar) and C (the reader takes the page) are deleted from this board; the README records them.
  • Being built into Pro's panel: markup in `Order_Pay_Panel.php`, behaviour in `order-pay-panel.js`, a new `assets/css/order-pay-panel.css`.

Every column follows the strip. Amounts and words are the app's strings.

What each column keeps from the 2026-10-05 decision

  • Words, not wire. Every row in Details uses the app's catalogue (`tender/labels.ts`): "Sent to Front Desk", "On the terminal, waiting for the customer", "Approved $153.71". Ids sit in mono under the row, never in the sentence. Today's panel prints the server event verbatim ("Reader action pi_… created on tmr_…").
  • The status is said once. One line, with a dot in the state's colour; the steps say where, the log says why.
  • Buttons are verbs with the object on them. "Send $153.71 to Front Desk" is the one primary; "Cancel on terminal" is outline-red and sits apart; "Try again" only after an end. Today shows all three at once, two of them greyed.
  • The reader is chosen with chips when there is more than one (as in the tender pane), not a select; with one reader there is nothing to choose.
  • Details hides until there is something to list, unfolds inside the row, and carries Copy. A refused request (today's 400) is a red line there, so it is never empty.
  • Precedents: Cash App, Brex and Too Good To Go status timelines (a tick per step, the time under); Square and Zettle's "Waiting for card" moment (one line, one cancel); Stripe's own Terminal reader card for the connected badge.
